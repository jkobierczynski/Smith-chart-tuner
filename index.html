What the chart is actually showing

The Smith chart is a polar plot of the complex reflection coefficient Γ = (Z − Z₀) / (Z + Z₀), redrawn with a grid of impedance instead of raw numbers. Every point inside the outer rim is an impedance: the curved grid lines are circles of constant resistance and arcs of constant reactance, so reading a point's position off the grid tells you R and X directly. The rim itself is |Γ| = 1 — total reflection, as seen by a dead short or a wide-open circuit. The exact center is Γ = 0: the load equals Z₀, nothing reflects, and the meter reads a flat 1.0.

Three things move you across that grid, and they look different. A length of feedline doesn't change what the antenna's impedance is — it only changes what it looks like from the other end, by rotating your point around the chart's center at constant radius (or, with real cable loss, spiralling slowly inward as energy leaks away each trip down and back). A series reactance only changes X and leaves R untouched, so it slides your point along one of the cyan constant-resistance circles. A shunt susceptance only changes conductance, so it slides you along one of the amber constant-conductance circles instead. Chain a series and a shunt step, in either order, and you can reach the center from almost any starting point: that's the whole reason an L-, Pi-, or T-network can match nearly any real-world antenna.

Feedpoint impedances for the presets are simplified, illustrative curves — not measured antenna data. Cable loss figures are similarly illustrative, not a manufacturer's spec.

Field NoteFeedline caveat

The tuner sits at the transmitter end of the cable. Tuning it only forces the impedance at that end back to 50 Ω, which is what the SWR meter is reading. The mismatch — and the standing waves — between the tuner and the antenna itself are completely unchanged; watch the "At the antenna" scope and the SWR at the feedline's antenna end stay stubbornly high even at a perfect 1.00 transmitter reading. A lossy line still burns extra power in those standing waves no matter how nicely this meter reads. An antenna tuner protects your transmitter, it doesn't repair your antenna.
